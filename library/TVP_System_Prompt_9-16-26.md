- Case et al. 2017: neuroimaging study of FtM transgender
  individuals. Using MEG and DTI, found significantly
  reduced sensory integration in brain areas associated
  with body ownership for gender-incongruent body parts,
  and heightened amygdala alarm response. White matter
  differences in supramarginal gyrus and medial temporal

- Morabito et al. 2021: case report of a 15-year-old whose
  gender dysphoria presented entirely as somatic symptoms —
  arthralgia, myalgia, headache, fatigue — for months before
  the underlying gender incongruence was identified. Documents
  how dysphoria can be masked by or expressed through
  physical symptoms, particularly in adolescents.
- Anger et al. 2024: comprehensive review of pain mechanisms
  in transgender individuals. Covers social stress as
  pro-inflammatory driver of pain, effects of hormone therapy
  on pain, post-surgical pain phenotypes, body dysphoria and
  pain interaction, and neuroimaging findings. Finds
  transgender people experience higher rates of pain,
  influenced by minority stress, hormone therapy, and
  gender-related body dysphoria.
- Nguyen et al. 2018: narrative review in Neuropsychopharmacology.
  Synthesizes neuroimaging and neurobiological research on transgender
  brains. Key finding: some brain differences in transgender individuals
  predate hormone treatment, supporting a neurobiological basis for
  gender identity. Covers hypothalamic structures (BSTc, INAH-3), white
  matter microstructure via DTI, functional neuroimaging, and cognitive
  effects of hormone therapy.
- Theisen et al. 2019 (Scientific Reports): whole exome sequencing
  study of 30 transgender individuals at Augusta University. Identified
  21 genetic variants in 19 genes associated with estrogen receptor
  activated pathways of sexually dimorphic brain development. Variants
  were absent in 88 non-transgender controls and not present in standard
  genetic databases. Findings are preliminary (small sample) but suggest
  a biological basis for gender identity rooted in prenatal brain
  development pathways.
- Swaab, Wolff & Bao 2021 (Handbook of Clinical Neurology, Ch. 31):
  comprehensive review of sexual differentiation of the human
  hypothalamus in relation to gender identity and sexual orientation.
  Synthesizes BSTc, INAH-3, and neuroimaging findings. Key conclusion:
  brain structure sizes, neuron numbers, molecular composition, and
  connectivity determine gender identity. No evidence that postnatal
  social environment plays a role. Covers hormones, genetics,
  epigenetics, endocrine disruptors, and immune response as mechanisms.
- Reiner & Gearhart 2004 (New England Journal of Medicine): study of

  Of 14 raised as girls, 8 later declared male identity despite female
  upbringing and surgically feminized anatomy. All 16 showed moderate-

  exposure programs gender identity in ways that social rearing cannot
  override.
- Diamond & Sigmundson 1997 (Archives of Pediatric and Adolescent
  Medicine): long-term follow-up of the John/Joan case (David Reimer).
  A genetic male whose penis was accidentally ablated was raised as
  female under John Money's direction. The individual rejected female
  identity throughout childhood, switched to living as male at puberty,
  and lived successfully as male into adulthood. Directly refutes
  Money's claim that gender identity is socially malleable and
  determined by rearing.

  Psychiatry): Largest genetic study of trans women at time of publication;

  Austria, Switzerland, Poland, and France. Distinguishes discontinuation
  from regret — they are separate concepts; discontinuation is not
  automatically a quality failure.
- Conabere, Ravine, Malta, Bourchier, Harte, Hopper & Pang 2025
  (Scientific Reports 15:21680): twin study plus meta-analysis of 463
  twin pairs. Concordance for a transgender or gender diverse identity

  on: concordance among same-sex DZ pairs (9.2%) and opposite-sex DZ
  pairs (8.2%) was statistically indistinguishable. Open access,
  NHMRC-funded, Murdoch Children's Research Institute.
